# Source quality policy

## Purpose

This policy prevents public marketing information, third-party search results, and
controlled engineering evidence from being treated as equivalent.

## Evidence levels

| Level | Meaning | Permitted use |
|---|---|---|
| `source-claimed` | A fact is published by the company or another named source but has not been checked against controlled evidence. | Discovery and preliminary comparison |
| `externally-corroborated` | An independent primary or clearly identified secondary source supports the fact. | Corporate/research context; not final product design |
| `verified` | An authorized reviewer checked the fact against a controlled drawing, specification, certificate, test record, or approved manual. | Use within the scope and revision of the cited evidence |

Google and other search engines are discovery tools, not evidence. Cite the page or
document reached through the search result, record the access date, and identify
whether the publisher is primary, secondary, or unknown.

## Technical-data rule

Do not promote size, pressure, material, leakage, torque, coating, test, or standard
edition data to `verified` solely because it appears on a web page or in a search
snippet. Record the controlled evidence reference and reviewer.

## Conflicts and stale information

When sources disagree:

1. Keep both claims and their dates.
2. Mark the record `draft`.
3. Prefer the applicable approved project document over public material.
4. Ask the document owner to resolve the conflict.
5. Preserve the decision and evidence in the change history.

## Copyright and confidentiality

Summarize public information and link to the publisher. Do not reproduce licensed
standards, full catalogues, drawings, customer documents, personal data, credentials,
or internal manufacturing parameters without written authorization.
